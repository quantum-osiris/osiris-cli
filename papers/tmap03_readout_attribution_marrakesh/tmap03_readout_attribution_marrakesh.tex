\title{\bfseries Four days later, the best qubits were the worst: a preregistered replication on
\texttt{ibm\_marrakesh} in which readout faults invisible to the published calibration reversed a
10\,pp layout effect}
\author{Devin Phillip Davis\\ \small Agile Defense Systems LLC}
\date{20 November 2026 \quad (TESSERACT-MAP-03 results, version 1.0)}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
TESSERACT-MAP-02 (16 November 2026) found that a 40-qubit circuit on a calibration-ranked ``best''
path of \texttt{ibm\_marrakesh} beat the same circuit on a median path by $+10.5$\,pp of classical
fidelity at an identical 39~CZ and depth~66. We preregistered and ran a follow-up on the same physical
qubits four days later (one job, 80 PUBs, \QpuSeconds{}\,s QPU; preregistration published on Zenodo
before submission). The effect did not replicate: it \emph{reversed}. The best path now scored
$-4.29$\,pp below the median path (95\% CI $[-5.24,-3.33]$, Holm $p=6.4\times10^{-6}$), and its
fidelity fell from \MtwoCfive{} to \Aone. Union readout calibration shows why. Qubit 46, on the best
path, reported 1 for a prepared $|0\rangle$ in \QFSixP\% of shots, while IBM's calibration at the
time of the job listed its readout error as \QFSixIBM\%. After tensored readout mitigation, the best
path again leads by \MitGap\,pp. The path effect also failed to carry over from the SWAP-free chain
to the routed star (interaction $-5.64$\,pp, $p=1.0\times10^{-6}$). Two conclusions are
supported. Layout rankings built on published calibration data can be overturned within days by
readout faults that the calibration does not show. And a cheap, per-job union readout check would
have caught it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background}
In MAP-02~\cite{map02}, at fixed gate count and depth, the choice of 40 physical qubits changed
classical fidelity $F_{\mathrm{cl}}$ by 10.5\,pp, and a greedy calibration-weighted path beat Qiskit's
stock level-3 layout by 5.7\,pp. Union readout calibration found two qubits on the losing layouts
(q41, q42) with 12--22\% assignment error that IBM listed near 1\%. MAP-02 left three questions open:
does the gap replicate, how much of it is readout, and is the path effect additive across circuit
structures? We preregistered MAP-03 to answer them~\cite{prereg}.

\section{Design}
The logical circuits are MAP-02's: a 40-qubit state with exact support on four strings, written
either as a degree-2 chain (39~CZ, no SWAPs) or as a degree-20 star routed by SABRE (79~CZ). All arms
use optimisation level~3. To exclude any influence of today's calibration or of MAP-02's outcome on
qubit choice, arms A1, A2, A3 and A6 \emph{replay MAP-02's frozen layouts} from its published lock:
A1 = chain on the best path (C5's qubits), A2 = chain on the median path (C6), A3 = chain on C7's
qubits, A6 = star on the best path (C2). A4 is the chain with the stock level-3 layout chosen on
today's calibration. A5, star on the median path, is new: it completes a $2\times2$ of structure by path.
Two union circuits prepare all-$|0\rangle$ and all-$|1\rangle$ on all \UnionN{} qubits any arm uses. Ten
shuffled blocks $\times$ 8 circuits $\times$ 2{,}048 shots ran in one job.

\paragraph{Endpoints and tests.} The primary endpoint is $F_{\mathrm{cl}}$, scored as in MAP-02. The
readout-mitigated $F_{\mathrm{cl}}$ applies the tensored inverse of each qubit's $2\times2$ assignment
matrix, with rates pooled from the union circuits over all blocks, evaluated on the four ideal strings.
There are five contrasts, each a paired $t$-test over 10 blocks (df~$=9$) with Holm correction:
K1 = A1$-$A2 (path gap);
K2 = raw gap $-$ mitigated gap (readout share);
K3 = (A1$-$A2)$-$(A6$-$A5) (structure $\times$ path interaction);
K4 = A1$-$A3; and K5 = A1$-$A4. The decision rule for K2 was conditional on K1 replicating.

\paragraph{Provenance.} Locked \LockedUtc{}Z. The preregistration, lock, QPY and analysis code were
published as Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.24406496 at 2026-11-20 20:13:37Z (Zenodo server
time). Job \texttt{\JobId} was submitted at \SubmittedUtc{}Z. Before submission, a noiseless
matrix-product-state simulation of every compiled arm gave $F_{\mathrm{cl}}\ge0.9997$, and the
mitigation restored $F_{\mathrm{cl}}\approx1$ on synthetic readout-only data.

\paragraph{K1: the path gap reversed.} The chain on MAP-02's best path scored $-4.29$\,pp
\emph{below} the median path, and every block agreed. By the preregistered rule the gap did not
replicate. It also changed sign significantly. The best path's $F_{\mathrm{cl}}$ halved, from
\MtwoCfive{} to \Aone, while the median path's rose from 0.110 to 0.144.

\paragraph{Why: readout.} Three qubits on the best path read out far worse than four days earlier
(Table~\ref{tab:qubits}). q46, on the best path, gave $p(1|0)=\QFSixP\%$ and $p(0|1)=\QFSixQ\%$;
in MAP-02 its mean assignment error was 1.3\%, and IBM's lock-time and execution-time calibrations
list about 1\%. q31, on both paths, gave $p(0|1)=\QTOneQ\%$, and q37 rose from 1.9\% to 13.6\%. The q46 fault is stable across blocks
and appears independently in the circuit data: in A1, q46's bit disagrees with the majority of the 19
qubits that ideally share its value in 39\% of shots. MAP-02's outliers have largely recovered:
q41 is at 1.6\% and q42 at 4.8\%.

\paragraph{K2: readout share.} The preregistered rule for K2 applied only if K1 replicated, so we report
K2 descriptively. After mitigation the best path leads by \MitGap\,pp (mitigated $F_{\mathrm{cl}}$ 0.651
against 0.481). The mitigation therefore moves the gap by 21.3\,pp ($p=1.8\times10^{-6}$), from a
4.3\,pp deficit to a 17.0\,pp lead. The preregistered share $\eta=1-\text{mitigated}/\text{raw}$ assumes
both gaps have the same sign. Here it comes out at \EtaEst{} and is not interpretable as a fraction.
On the mitigated endpoint the MAP-02 ranking holds: the best path is better on everything except
readout.

\paragraph{K3: no additivity.} For the routed star, the best path still beat the median path
(+1.35\,pp), the opposite sign to the chain. The interaction is $-5.64$\,pp ($p=1.0\times10^{-6}$).
SABRE routing changes which logical bit each physical qubit holds at measurement, and the star has
79 CZ, so the same faulty qubit costs the two structures differently. We do not separate these
mechanisms.

\paragraph{K4, K5: other layouts.} MAP-02's stock-layout qubits (A3), which lost by 5.7\,pp on 16
November, now beat the best path by 5.26\,pp. Today's stock level-3 layout (A4) beat it by 1.75\,pp,
even though A4 also contains q46: IBM lists q46 at about 1\%, so the stock pass had no reason to avoid
it.

\section{Discussion}
MAP-02's central result, a 10.5\,pp layout effect at fixed gate count, was real for its calibration
window and gone four days later. What changed is not visible in IBM's published calibration. It
shows up only in an independent readout check run inside the same job. Two practical consequences
follow. First, rankings of physical qubits, whether by a greedy path search, VF2Layout or anything
else that consumes published calibration data, can be overturned within days by readout faults on
single qubits. Second, a union $|0\rangle$/$|1\rangle$ readout check costs two circuits per block and
would have flagged q46 before any conclusion was drawn. We recommend it, or a per-job readout probe,
as standard practice for layout studies.

The mitigated endpoint suggests that the gate and coherence quality ranking of MAP-02's paths
persisted. That rests on inverting a 54\% assignment error, which amplifies statistical noise by about
$3\times$ for that qubit, and on assuming that readout errors are independent between qubits. Both
caveats apply.

\paragraph{Limitations.} One job; one device; two dates. The mitigation is tensored (uncorrelated) and
uses all-0/all-1 calibration only. The K3 interaction is measured but not explained.
